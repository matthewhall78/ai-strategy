Common digital capabilities

Shared problems should create shared solutions.

Across government, teams often solve the same problems separately. Common capabilities create value when they reduce duplication, support adoption, and stay anchored in real service delivery needs.

1 · Shared problems across government

Many teams face the same core service problems.

When every team has to build its own solution, public servants spend energy rebuilding foundations instead of improving services. Shared problems are a signal that a common capability may be valuable.

🏛️

Many teams

Different ministries, programs, and service teams encounter similar delivery needs.

🔁

Same problems

Forms, permissions, approvals, notifications, data collection, routing, reporting.

🧩

Separate builds

Teams create local tools, custom processes, and one-off patterns.

⚡

Opportunity

Solve the problem once, then scale the benefit through reuse and adoption.

2 · Consequences of disconnected solutions

Disconnected solutions create system-level drag.

The cost is not just technical. It shows up as slower delivery, unclear user experiences, growing backlogs, and higher support effort across government.

Duplicated work +
Multiple teams rebuild the same basic capability instead of investing once in a shared foundation.

Growing backlogs +
Feature requests accumulate when teams try to satisfy every local need without a clear shared problem definition.

Inconsistent services +
Users encounter different patterns for similar tasks, increasing friction and reducing trust.

Rising cost +
More tools, integrations, custom support paths, and duplicated maintenance increase whole-of-government cost.

4 · The discipline
Measure success by solving problems, not completing a list of features.

Each feature is a bet that it will help teams solve a specific shared problem. Protecting the problem definition makes it easier to say no, stay focused, and explain decisions clearly.

5 · Shared problems create shared solutions

Reuse works when a few committed teams solve one real problem together.

You do not need hundreds of contributors. A small number of committed teams, with a simple way to collaborate, can create a capability that saves effort across the system.

6 · Designing for different users without product bloat

Shared does not mean one-size-fits-all.

Platform thinking helps teams support different contexts without creating duplicate products or letting every use case reshape the core. The goal is purposeful variation on top of a stable foundation.

🧱

Stable foundation

Common infrastructure, rules, integrations, and service patterns reduce the effort required to start.

🛤️

Purposeful paths

Different users can have different interfaces or workflows when their needs are genuinely different.

🧭

Clear boundaries

Product teams protect what the capability is for, what it is not for, and where local variation belongs.

7 · Actions by role

Everyone has a role in keeping shared capabilities focused on outcomes.

Hover each card. The shared discipline is the same: protect the problem definition, support reuse, and measure value through adoption and real-world use.

👥

Executives

Focus: protect and scale
Fund small, permanent platform teams.
Measure adoption, reuse, and saved effort.
Choose service patterns that support variation without duplication.
📍

Product Owners

Focus: protect the problem
Build roadmaps around shared problems, not feature volume.
Treat each new feature as a test of value.
Encourage reuse through consistent service patterns.
🔎

Scrum Masters

Focus: expose drift
Keep teams focused on why features exist.
Surface shared problems across teams.
Name scope creep when it appears as “one more helpful thing.”
🛠️

Developers & UX

Focus: design for use
Start with outcomes, then choose the right delivery pattern.
Support purposeful interface differences.
Make systems simple to start, easy to leave, and easy to integrate.
Closing message

Common capabilities become valuable when they feel less like overhead and more like leverage.

When teams are trusted to choose their tools, focus on shared problems, and design intentionally for different contexts, common capabilities become a force multiplier for better public services.
